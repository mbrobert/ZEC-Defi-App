Privacy

Exactly what an observer can and cannot see. Every claim below was probed against the live NEAR Intents API on 2026-08-08 — not taken from documentation.

The three legs

Privacy is not one switch — it differs per hop. We default to the most private path available at each one.

1 · Your ZEC in History stays private
The bridge issues a fresh transparent deposit address for every deposit. Spending to it from your shielded pool is an ordinary deshielding transaction — Zcash never reveals where shielded funds came from.
Private: your balance, your address, your entire transaction history. Nothing links backwards. Public: the amount, and that some shielded holder funded this one address.
2 · The strategy itself Public — unavoidably
Once bridged, your ZEC is a token on NEAR, collateral on Rhea, and an LP position on Base. Those are transparent ledgers. We will not dress this up: position size, health factor, pool, rebalances and claims are all visible.
What still protects you: an observer sees a position, not a person — unless leg 3 hands them the link.
3 · ZEC back to you Public on arrival, then shielded
The bridge settles to transparent addresses only — we verified that u1… and zs1… recipients are rejected today. Funds land in the open, then your wallet shields them; most do it automatically.
Your job: a fresh receiving address per position, shielded on arrival. We track this and warn you.

What actually deanonymizes people

The bridge is not the weak point. These are, in order of real-world impact.

Reusing one receiving address. Every position paid to the same address is publicly linked to every other one, and to you. Highest-impact mistake by far — we detect it across your positions and flag it.
Reusing your deposit-refund address for rewards. Joins the inbound and outbound legs directly, connecting your Zcash identity to the whole position.
Amount and timing correlation. A distinctive amount leaving a shielded pool and the same amount appearing on Base moments later correlates the two regardless of address hygiene. Round amounts and elapsed time help; this one has real residual risk and we would rather say so.
Shielding rewards back into the account you deposited from. Fine for most threat models; weak against an adversary already watching that account.

Getting funds back into the shielded pool

Payouts land transparent. Here is exactly how they become private again — and why we never do it for you.

Your wallet shields it — usually by itself
Zashi (now Zodl) shields ZEC it receives, so a payout is exposed only briefly. Its unified address is shielded-only since 2.0.3, which is why we ask for the separate transparent address on its Receive screen. Other wallets expose a shield action to run once funds land.
Payouts are always large enough to be worth shielding
Shielding costs a small network fee, and wallets skip dust. The agent already batches rewards until they clear 3× the cost of claiming, with a $5 floor — comfortably above any auto-shield threshold. That floor is doing double duty; lowering it would start stranding dust in the open.
We cannot shield on your behalf — by design
Shielding is a transaction signed by whoever holds the keys. For us to do it, we would have to custody your ZEC. We do not, and will not. What we can do is route the payout to a wallet that shields itself, and tell you precisely how — which is what the deposit flow does.

Getting started

You need two things: a Zcash wallet (Zodl, Ywallet, Zingo — any wallet that sends ZEC) and, for Full-strategy positions, an EVM wallet on Base (MetaMask is fine) that authorizes your withdrawals. Your Zcash wallet never connects to anything — it just sends and receives ZEC.

Want a single app for both sides? Unstoppable Wallet holds shielded ZEC and connects to Base dApps via WalletConnect. It's the only mainstream wallet that does both.

Your first deposit, in three steps

1 · Pick an amount and a mode in New deposit — the defaults are chosen to be sane, and everything advanced stays folded away until you want it.

2 · Review the projection rail: yield estimate, health factor, liquidation price, fees, and a live privacy grade. Nothing moves until you confirm.

3 · Scan the deposit QR with your wallet (amount pre-filled via ZIP-321) and send. The progress view tracks every hop with a verifiable transaction id per step.

There is no account, no email, no KYC. Your position is keyed to addresses you control.

The three modes

Simple lending

ZEC supplied as collateral on Rhea Finance (NEAR). Earns supply APY that accrues in-protocol automatically — there is nothing to compound and nothing to manage. No borrow, no liquidation exposure. Upgrades to Full strategy in place, without re-bridging.

Full strategy

Borrow USDC, cbBTC or WETH against your ZEC (≤50% LTV), bridge to Base via NEAR Intents, and deploy into a managed concentrated-liquidity position with your exact parameters. Your ZEC is never sold — that's the point. Borrowing against an asset is how holders earn on it without a disposal.

Direct LP

Swap ZEC straight into the pool assets and LP — no loan, no health factor, no liquidation risk. The trade-off: the swap itself is a disposal of your ZEC (tax treatment varies by jurisdiction — not advice, just mechanics), and you take the LP's price exposure instead of ZEC's.

Deposits & funding

Every deposit gets a freshly generated address — deposits are never linked to each other by a shared address. Send from your shielded balance (recommended, default) and your history stays private; the app shows a ZIP-321 QR your wallet scans with the amount pre-filled.

Why can't the amount field read my balance?

No Zcash wallet exposes a connection a web app can query, and shielded balances are unreadable by third parties by design — that's the privacy you're here for. You type the amount; the QR carries it into your wallet.

The 60-second hold

The LP engine enforces a 60s minimum hold after every deposit into a position (flash-loan/JIT defense) — including compounds and the internal re-deposit behind a partial withdrawal. The UI shows the countdown; the agent handles retries.

Health & liquidations

Your health factor is account-level: all your ZEC collateral and all your debt on Rhea pool into one number. HF = collateral × 70% ÷ debt. No debt = infinite HF, shown as ∞.

Hard liquidation (Rhea's — the thing we keep you away from)

At HF 1.0, third-party liquidators repay part of your debt and take collateral at a discount. You pay that discount, at the worst price, at the worst time.

The agent's soft ladder (ours — runs first)

HF ≤ 1.5 — warn + notify; new borrows blocked. HF ≤ 1.35 — all claimable rewards route to debt repayment instead of compounding; principal untouched. HF ≤ 1.2 — minimal unwind: sell exactly enough LP to restore HF 1.6 (repay = debt − collateral×0.7÷1.6), starting with the most out-of-range slice. HF ≤ 1.05 — emergency full unwind, slippage floors still on.

The app also refuses collateral withdrawals that would push post-withdraw HF below 1.2 — stricter than the protocol's 1.0 on purpose.

Rewards & claiming

Every position sits staked in its pool's Aerodrome gauge. While staked, the pool's trading fees go to veAERO voters — your position earns AERO incentives instead (at equilibrium, emissions track fees; that's the deal that makes gauges work). Claimable is the AERO accrued, net of fees — trading fees are never part of it. Every position has Compound now (re-deposit into the position, works whether or not auto-compound is on) and Claim, with three destinations:

1 · Your Zcash wallet — converted via NEAR Intents, arrives as native ZEC. The quote is hard-verified before send: recipient must equal your stored address, asset must be native ZEC, quote hash emitted on-chain.
2 · Your Rhea position — repays debt first (raises your health factor; usually the smartest choice while borrowing), surplus tops up collateral.
3 · Your Base wallet — raw reward tokens, no conversion.

Claim economics — why we don't claim constantly

Claiming costs gas (+ bridge cost for ZEC routing). The agent claims when rewards ≥ 3× the cost, with a $5 floor and a 30-day max hold. Example: costs $1.80 → trigger is $5.40. The $5 floor doubles as privacy hygiene: payouts always land big enough that your wallet shields them rather than stranding dust in the open.

What triggers auto-compound

Not a timer: the same economics gate, checked every agent cycle — plus a free ride on every rebalance, since rewards get collected then anyway.

Withdrawals

Never pausable. Not by us, not by anyone — it's a contract invariant, not a policy.

Withdrawals offer the same three destinations as claims: repay your loan (default while you have debt — you see the HF before/after), home as native ZEC, or tokens to your Base wallet. Withdrawn LP funds are never silently re-supplied as collateral.

The slippage floor

Before any exit we compute what the position should return from current pool state and set minOut just under it. If an MEV sandwich would push your proceeds below the floor, the transaction reverts instead of eating the loss. This exists because our fork tests measured a >1% round-trip loss on an oversized position in a thin pool — the floor plus per-pool caps are the fix.

Partial withdrawals

The engine has no partial-exit call, so the adapter closes and re-opens the remainder — which restarts the 60s hold on what stays.

The full boundary map — what's private, what's public, at every hop, with live per-position grades — lives in the Privacy tab. The short version: fund from your shielded balance and your history stays private; the strategy legs (NEAR, Base) are public, and we say so instead of dressing it up; payouts land transparent and your wallet shields them on arrival.

The rules that matter most, learned from real deanonymizations of other products: fresh receiving address per position (we detect reuse and warn), never reuse your refund address for rewards, and payout sizes stay above dust so they always get shielded.

Risks & venue status

Plainly, the risks you take: ZEC price risk on collateral (liquidation exists — see Health), impermanent loss inside LP ranges, and smart-contract risk across Rhea, NEAR Intents and the LP engine. Diversification across those layers is deliberate but does not make any of them zero.

Rhea Finance — stated openly

Rhea was exploited for $18.4M in April 2026 (margin-engine validation flaw; post-mortems by Halborn and others; funds substantially recovered per reporting). What we do about depending on it: per-venue exposure caps, the agent independently monitors collateral/borrow invariants rather than trusting venue numbers, and our kill-switch can pause new deposits only — user withdrawals are exempt from pause at the contract level, always.

Structural protections

Principal never routes through the reward path. The operator can manage yield but can never redirect principal. Per-pool caps keep any position a small share of pool liquidity so exits stay clean. Every reward route home emits its quote hash on-chain for auditability.

FAQ

Does my ZEC get sold?

Simple lending and Full strategy: never — it sits as collateral and comes back as ZEC. Direct LP: yes, that mode explicitly swaps it (that's the trade-off it exists to offer).

Why does my new position show a 60-second cooldown?

The engine's flash-loan defense. Every deposit restarts it, including compounds. It's cosmetic in normal use.

Why do you need a transparent (t1…) address for payouts?

The bridge settles to transparent addresses only today — we verified this against the live API (unified and Sapling recipients are rejected). Your wallet shields on arrival; if shielded settlement ships, one flag flips and shielded payouts become the default end to end.

What's the engine pool id on each pool?

The exact bytes32 key our deposit passes to the engine's registry — shown so you can verify the pool you picked is the pool you got, on-chain, yourself.

Can you freeze my funds?

No. Withdrawal is exempt from pause in the contract. The operator role can manage yield strategy, never move principal anywhere but back to you.

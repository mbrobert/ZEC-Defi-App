## Scope 8 · Slice F — the direct venue, the adapter, the router, the keeper, the web

### W3-MED-1 — keeper protection could never be granted for a cbZEC/USDC position

**Severity.** Medium. The product's headline pool; every user of it, from the first open.

**file:line (on `68d8037`).** `web/lib/execute.ts:359-366` (`grantPoolTokenPricing`: price =
Aave reserve or `NaN`), `:336-340` (`grantTokenLimits` throws on a non-finite price);
`web/app/new/page.tsx:136-143` (the wizard's grant step builds limits from
`grantPoolTokenPricing([token0, token1], market)`); `web/app/dashboard/page.tsx:225` (the
re-grant, the same call). On chain: `contracts/src/swap/SlipstreamPoolSwapAdapter.sol:145-154`
(the callback pays the pool by `transfer` from the account) and
`contracts/src/account/OilskinAccount.sol:547-580` (`_decodeTokenOp` charges it) — a keeper unwind
on the direct pool NEEDS a cbZEC budget line.

**Invariant broken.** "A position opened with keeper protection can be protected" (`RISKS.md`
§10; the wizard's Simple mode promises the grant). Wave 2's G-HIGH-1 fix made a token without a
USD price refuse the grant rather than sign a wrong line — correct — but the only price source was
Aave's oracle, and cbZEC has no Aave reserve.

**Failing scenario (executed).** `web/test/execute.test.ts` W3-MED-1: `grantPoolTokenPricing(["USDC",
"cbZEC"], DEMO_MARKET)` prices cbZEC `NaN`; `grantTokenLimits(30_000, cbBTC, that)` throws
`no USD price for cbZEC`. In the product: the wizard's grant step emits "The keeper permission was
not built … grant it later from the dashboard" and the open proceeds without protection; the
dashboard's re-grant fails the same way, forever. The keeper, for its part, refuses the dispatch
by name on that account (`SlipstreamLpVenue.t.sol` `test_keeperUnwindOnTheDirectPoolIsBudgeted`:
`TokenNotBudgeted(cbZEC)`), so the account under the ladder is never acted on.

**Impact.** Every leveraged cbZEC/USDC position runs without the keeper, while the UI's language
("grant it later") implies a temporary state. No funds move; the position is closable by the
owner.

**Fix.** Above (fix-round table); commit `1fe4a41`. The pool's own USDC price sizes the line: a grant line is a CAP
in the token's units (2 × debt), not a valuation, so the pool spot is an honest size for it, and
it is refreshed every time the grant is built.

### W3-MED-2 — a stranger's NFTs switch the keeper off for a direct-venue account

**Severity.** Medium. Griefing, cheap (512 dust mints and transfers on Base), targeted at any
account with a direct-venue position; no funds taken.

**file:line (on `68d8037`).** `contracts/src/venues/SlipstreamLpVenue.sol:334-366` (`positionsOf`:
`staked.length + held > MAX_ENUMERATION` → `TooManyPositions`, with `held = NPM.balanceOf(account)`
— every Slipstream token the account holds, any pool, before filtering); consumers:
`agent/src/dispatch/keeperDispatcher.ts` `readLpState` (a revert → the dispatch is REFUSED "cannot
read LP state (fail closed)"), `web/lib/reads.ts` `readAccount` (→ "positions unreadable").

**Invariant broken.** "Cannot enumerate is never reported as owns nothing" was slice A's rule, and
this venue kept it — but the fail-closed answer was reachable by a third party. The engine venue
has no such door (engine ids are minted to the depositor); ERC-721 transfers are permissionless.

**Failing scenario (executed, before the fix).** One staked position (`open` 10,000 USDC); a
griefer mints 512 dust positions in the pool (`MintParams` at ticks −24,000/−22,800, 2 USDC + 0.01
cbZEC each) and `transferFrom`s them to the account; `positionsOf(account)` reverts
`TooManyPositions(512)`. The keeper's next tick for that account REFUSES every rung; the dashboard
shows "positions unreadable … The keeper reads the same list and does not act on it either."

**Impact.** Keeper protection off for the targeted account for as long as the tokens sit there
(the owner can burn them, at their own gas). The account's own staked position was never at risk
from the tokens themselves — only from the protection they switched off.

**Fix.** Above; commit `1fe4a41`. The staked list is the account's own and always whole; the unstaked scan is a
window; the overflow is named on both readers. *Residual, stated:* an unstaked position the
account itself holds (a gauge-dead open, or a `closeMany` that unstaked but could not withdraw)
can sit beyond the window when a stranger pads the holdings, and is then invisible to the keeper
and the dashboard until the owner clears the padding — the owner's raw exec reaches it regardless.

### W3-LOW-1 — the same numeric id on both venues routes the unwind to the engine's

### W3-LOW-2 — the to-ratio swap's tolerance collapses to zero at the band's edge

**Status.** **Fixed** (the W3-LOW-2 commit): `SlipstreamLpVenue.toRatioToleranceBps(band)` — half
the band's own price span, `(1 − (min / max)²) / 2`, capped at the adapter's ceiling, wherever inside
the band the price sits; `_swap` uses it. `contracts/test/audit-regressions/DirectVenueBandEdge.t.sol`
(4): an open with the price on the band's lower edge and a 0.1 % impact opens (on `fabf8b6` it
reverted `InsufficientOutput`), the mirror at the upper edge selling token1, the tolerance's values
(±1 % band → 197 bps in the venue's integer arithmetic, ±10 % → the 500 cap, a point band → 0), and the cap still refusing 6 %.

**Severity.** Low (liveness, no loss). `contracts/src/venues/SlipstreamLpVenue.sol:508-527`
(`_swap`: `keepBps` from the band's edge relative to the CURRENT price; at the edge `tol = 0`, so
any price impact fails `InsufficientOutput`). An open quoted at the centre of a ±1 % band whose
price drifts to the edge before execution reverts instead of opening; the user re-quotes. **Cost:**
a policy number — a floor on the tolerance (say 20 bps) would be a product number this repo does
not have; the alternative is to derive the tolerance from the band's half-width rather than from
the current price (≈ 5 lines), which lets the swap use the whole band the user signed.

`contracts/src/oracle/PythOracleAdapter.sol:136-154` (`twapPriceE8`): the tick average floors toward

of `expo`. `_deviationBps` uses Pyth as the reference. Two residuals:

### W3-INFO-7 — a thin pool's TWAP can be pushed into `PegBreak` on purpose

A mover who holds the cbZEC/USDC tick ≈ 300 ticks away for the whole `twapWindow` (1,800 s), or
proportionally further for less time, trips the 3 % bound and `price()` reverts — for the market's
liquidations too. Against a ≈ $0.9M pool that is expensive to sustain against arbitrage, and it is
a DoS of a fail-closed oracle, not a mispricing. A product decision for v1.1 (a longer window, or
a second source) — not made here.

### W3-INFO-8 — `observe` needs the pool's observation cardinality to cover the window

If the pool's cardinality does not reach 1,800 s back, `observe` reverts ("OLD") and so does
`price()` — fail closed, and a liveness item for the v1.1 deploy checklist (grow the cardinality,
or read it in the deploy guard).

**Halmos properties written (compile under Forge; not picked up by `forge test`).**
`contracts/test/halmos/AccountGrant.halmos.t.sol` — every parameter of a `check_` function is a
symbol over its whole type, so each is a statement about every value of that shape:

1. `check_ungrantedRootCallAlwaysReverts(target, selector, word)` — with one grant on
   `(token, transfer)`, a keeper root call to ANY other `(target, selector)` reverts `NotGranted`:
   no calldata reaches outside the signed Permission.
2. `check_transferIsChargedExactlyAndNeverPastBudget(limit, amount, to)` — a recognised mover is
   charged exactly the amount in its calldata, `spent ≤ limit` always, and `amount > limit`
   always reverts.
3. `check_unbudgetableMoversAlwaysRevert(which, a, b, c)` — the five movers the parser cannot read
   revert `UnbudgetableSelector` for every argument, even under a grant on that very selector.
4. `check_nonMoverIsNeverCharged(selector, a, b)` — any selector that is not one of the nine
   consumes no token budget, for every payload.

`contracts/test/halmos/RouterBalance.halmos.t.sol` —
`check_unwindLeavesRouterBalanceUnchanged(repay, withdraw, dust)`: after a book is opened on Aave
and `dust` is donated to the router, `unwind` with any repay and withdraw amount either reverts or
leaves the router's USDC and cbBTC balances exactly where they were. This one walks the whole
unwind tree (the registry's venue list, nested calls into four mocks) and is the bounded attempt:
**expected to time out rather than to prove or refute** at `--loop 3`; CI runs it under
`continue-on-error` and a 20-minute timeout so a timeout is recorded, never mistaken for a pass or
a failure.

**What is out of halmos's reach here, stated.** The engine venue's enumeration (a bounded loop of

fork. The account properties (1–4) are the ones symbolic execution is good at: a handful of

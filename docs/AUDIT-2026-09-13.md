### S-1 · Medium · The bridge stall window could never expire

**Where:** `agent/src/solana/monitor.ts`, `bridgeInFlightAgeS`.

**What it was.** The age of a burn in flight was measured from the dispatch record's `updatedAt`. The monitor
re-enters a SENT record on every tick and `recordResult` rewrites `updatedAt` each time, so the age read
approximately zero for ever.

**The failing scenario.** A cross-chain rung fires; the burn lands on Base; Circle's attestation service is
down for six hours. Every tick, `bridgeDecision` is asked whether to act and is told a burn has been in flight
for about 0 seconds — inside the 1,800-second stall window — so it answers `wait`. The account waits for the
whole outage with its health factor falling, when the fallback that exists for exactly this case (the
keeper-funded sale on Solana, which needs no other chain) would have protected it after thirty minutes. The
stall window, and the paragraph of the runbook describing it, were dead code.

**Fix.** Measure from `createdAt` — the moment the rung fired, which is the honest answer to "how long has this
rung gone unanswered". **Regression test:** `agent/test/solana-monitor.test.ts`, "a burn in flight ages from
when the rung FIRED, not from the last tick": a record whose `createdAt` is two hours old and whose `updatedAt`
is now must report about two hours.

### S-2 · Medium · A burn could authorise a CCTP fee of nearly the whole amount

**Where:** `contracts/src/router/StrategyRouter.sol`, `closeLpAndBurn`.

**What it was.** The only bound on `maxFee` was `maxFee < burned`. `maxFee` is the caller's parameter, and on
the protective path the caller is the keeper.

**The failing scenario.** A keeper key is compromised. Its grant bounds the USDC it may move per period, and
the router fixes the destination, so the design's claim is that the worst it can do is "churn within budgets".
It could instead call `closeLpAndBurn` with `maxFee = burned − 1`: a real burn, inside the budget, to the
user's own recorded account — but authorising Circle to take all but one unit as the delivery fee. The user's
USDC would leave Base and essentially none of it would arrive. The claim was therefore not quite true.

**Fix.** `MAX_CCTP_FEE_BPS = 100` (1 %), refused by name as `MaxFeeTooLarge(maxFee, cap)`. Circle's own Fast
Transfer minimum on this route was **1.3 basis points** when it was read (`VERIFIED-SOLANA-FACTS.md`
Addendum 1), so the cap is two orders of magnitude above the real fee and binds only abuse. **Regression
test:** `contracts/test/audit-regressions/CctpFeeCap.t.sol` — the greedy fee and one unit over the cap are both
refused with nothing burned; the cap itself and Circle's real fee both pass and the bound reaches the message;
and the keeper cannot reach it through its grant.

## Observations (no code change; each is a judgement, recorded so it is a decision and not an oversight)

**O-1 · A bridged rung crosses the whole need while the reserve sits unspent.** At rungs 3–4 the keeper sizes
the burn as the full USDC the Solana debt needs, without subtracting the idle USDC the account already holds.
That idle USDC is the reserve, which §14.3 holds back precisely so that rung 2 stays atomic on Solana. Spending
it at rung 3 would protect faster but leave nothing for the rung the reserve exists for; crossing the full need
closes more LP than the minimum. Both are defensible and the current choice is the documented one. It is
recorded because the trade-off should be re-made once the Monte Carlo has sized the reserve, not left implicit.

**O-2 · An entry HF is not bounded above.** `ladderFor` accepts any entry at or above 1.10 and derives rungs
from it. A position that borrows just over the dust threshold against large collateral records an entry HF in
the millions, and its derived ladder is proportionally absurd — a "repay" rung at a health factor no position
could ever be in danger at. It cannot fire in practice (the position would have to fall 36 % to reach it, at
which point it is still unlevered), and the grant's budgets bound any action, so nothing is at risk. A clamp —
above some entry, run the floor's ladder — would be tidier, and `ladderForRecorded` is the one place to put it.

**O-3 · The keeper pays for the delivery.** `receive_message` creates the used-nonce account, and the keeper is
the payer as well as the fee payer. Rent is small and recoverable by Circle's program, but the keeper spends
SOL on the user's behalf with no accounting anywhere, and a keeper that runs out of SOL stops delivering while
looking healthy in every other respect. Worth a balance check and an alert before any real deployment.

**O-4 · `setSolanaRecipient` cannot verify the recipient.** The router stores 32 opaque bytes; nothing on Base
can prove they are the caller's own Solana token account rather than a stranger's. The keeper does check the
link is mutual before bridging (`pair.ts`), which is the real protection, but a user who sets the wrong value
by hand and then burns through the owner path sends their USDC somewhere else permanently. The web must derive
this value rather than accept it typed, and the deposit flow should show what it derived.

**O-5 · A mismatched message is refused for ever, quietly after the first time.** If Circle ever returned a
message that is not our burn, the rung FAILS and the error is logged — correct. But the record is retried and
the mismatch is re-logged each tick until the attempt cap abandons it. That is the right behaviour for a
transient fault and the wrong noise for a permanent one. A mismatch is closer to permanent than not, and could
be marked so.

> **Fixed 2026-09-16** (the commit that removed O-5 from `docs/BACKLOG.md`). `SolanaDispatchResult`'s `FAILED`
> arm gains `permanent?`, the same word its `REFUSED` arm already carried; `advanceBridge` sets it on
> `mismatch`; and `recordResult` gives a permanent FAILED the treatment a permanent REFUSED already got —
> straight to ABANDONED with the `permanent:` prefix the resume path reads, and one escalation on the tick it
> happened rather than after `maxDispatchAttempts` of them. **Why it is safe to call permanent:** Circle's
> attestation service is deterministic about a nonce, so the message it returns for one is the message it keeps
> returning; asking again cannot produce a different answer. **Regression tests:**
> `agent/test/solana-monitor.test.ts`, "a PERMANENT failure is ABANDONED on the first tick and never retried"
> (verified red with the new arm removed, green with it); and `agent/test/bridge-stages.test.ts`, where the
> mismatch case asserts `permanent` and a companion case asserts the failures retrying *can* fix — a record
> with no nonce, an unreachable service — are **not** marked permanent, so the flag cannot widen into a way to
> abandon a rung during an outage.

**O-6 · The reserve is a burn-time check only.** `deposit_for_burn` refuses a burn that would leave the account
under the reserve, but `transfer_out` is deliberately ungated (§14.3: the always-exit path), so the owner can
move the reserve to their wallet immediately afterwards. This is the documented design — the reserve is the
user's money and an exit must never be blocked — and the keeper reports the shortfall rather than preventing
it. Recorded so that no later reader mistakes the gap for an omission.

# Part 2 — the keeper's feed staleness policy (same day, separate scope)

**Scope:** `agent/src/engine/feeds.ts` and `agent/src/services/chain.ts` — the per-feed staleness bound the
keeper derives at startup. This is Base-module code that no pass since C-HIGH-2 had re-examined, and it was
re-opened by the ZEC/USD cadence read of `docs/VERIFIED-BASE-FACTS.md` Addendum 17. Reviewed at `d1223ef`;
the fix is in the commit this section lands in.

**Method:** the rule was replayed against the feeds' own published history — 1,000 rounds of ZEC/USD, 600
each of ETH/USD and cbBTC/USD, read read-only from Base — at every possible keeper start-up point, rather
than reasoned about. One defect was found.

| # | Severity | Where | One line |
|---|---|---|---|
| FEED-MED-1 | **Medium** | `agent/src/engine/feeds.ts`, `agent/src/services/chain.ts` | The staleness bound was measured over a count of rounds, not a window of time, so on a live feed it lands under that feed's own heartbeat and the keeper stops protecting |

### FEED-MED-1 · Medium · The staleness probe measured volatility, not cadence

**Where:** `agent/src/engine/feeds.ts:87` `buildFeedPolicies` (the `rounds` option, default 6 in
`agent/src/config.ts`) and `agent/src/services/chain.ts:224` `readRoundHistory`.

**What it was.** C-HIGH-2 replaced one global `PRICE_MAX_AGE_S` with a bound measured from each feed's own
published cadence: `max(largest gap over the last 6 rounds) × 2`, floored at 300 s. The principle is right —
"a heartbeat is measured, not declared" — and the window was wrong. A Chainlink feed publishes on two
triggers, a deviation threshold and a heartbeat, and **six rounds sampled while the market is moving contain
only deviation-driven gaps**, which are a fraction of the heartbeat. The derived bound is then a measure of
recent volatility wearing a cadence's clothes.

**The failing scenario, with the numbers from the feed itself.** ETH/USD on Base publishes on a **1,232 s
heartbeat** and has a **360 s median gap** (Addendum 17). A keeper started during an active hour walks back
six rounds, sees five gaps of roughly 300 s, and enforces a bound of about 600 s. Nothing is wrong yet: the
latest round is fresh, so the startup self-check passes and the daemon reports a healthy policy. The market
then calms. The feed falls back to its heartbeat, and **every publication is now 1,232 s apart against a
600 s bound** — so for 632 s out of every 1,232 s the WETH row reads stale, the account evaluates UNKNOWN,
and the ladder does not run. A position falling through its rungs during a quiet market is not defended.
Replayed over the real history, **34.7 % of all possible start-up points derive a bound below ETH/USD's own
heartbeat**, and at the 90th percentile the feed reads stale for **59.0 % of wall-clock time**. On ZEC/USD —
not wired today, but the feed the cbZEC market would use — the same probe reads stale **15.1 % of the time
at the median start-up**.

This is C-HIGH-2's failure reached by a different route: the constant was replaced, the blindness was not.
Two things kept it from being total — `unknownEscalationStreak` (3) tells the owner after three consecutive
UNKNOWN ticks, and cbBTC/USD happens to be nearly immune because its median gap already *is* its heartbeat
(1,230 s against 1,236 s), which is why the fixture-driven tests never caught it. Escalation reports the
blindness; it does not cure it, and the policy is derived once at startup and never revisited.

**Fix.** The probe samples a **window of time** with a **cap on calls**, not a count of rounds:
`readRoundHistory` walks back until the sample spans `minWindowS` (default **24 h**) or `maxRounds`
(default **120**, raised from 6) have been read. `FeedPolicy` now carries `windowCoveredS` and
`windowRounds`, both logged at startup. A walk that **could not** cover the window has not necessarily seen
a heartbeat, so its largest gap must not become a tight bound: that row is marked `probe-short` and takes
the fallback instead, loudly. `minWindowS: 0` asks for the old flat count deliberately, which is how the
regression test reproduces the defect. Measured over the same histories, the 90th-percentile stale time
falls from **59.04 % to 0.00 %** on ETH/USD and from **49.86 % to 4.48 %** on ZEC/USD; the round cap bounds
the startup call budget at 120 reads per feed, and the cbBTC and USDC bounds are unchanged.

The defaults were chosen from the measurement, not picked: 24 h / 120 is the smallest pair that takes the
90th-percentile stale time to 0.00 % on both wired feeds. A larger cap buys nothing.

**Regression test:** `agent/test/fixFEED1-heartbeat-window.test.ts` (5 tests) — the old six-round probe
derives 600 s against a 1,232 s heartbeat and calls a healthy round stale; the 24 h window sees the
heartbeat, bounds above it, and calls the same round fresh; a round four heartbeats late is still stale, so
widening the window has not disabled the guard; a feed too young to span the window reports `probe-short`
and takes the fallback; and a feed that never goes quiet stops at the cap. `agent/test/mockChain.ts` grew
`gapScheduleS` so a fixture can publish the way a real feed does — short deviation-driven gaps in an active
market, heartbeat-length ones behind them.

**What this does not fix.** The bound is still derived once, at startup. A feed whose cadence changes during
a long run is not re-probed; the escalation path is what catches that, and re-probing on a stale streak is a
judgement for a later slice, not a defect. And no measurement can bound a feed that has never shown its
heartbeat — for ZEC/USD the bound is still a volatility figure, which is precisely why Addendum 17 refuses
to derive `ChainlinkOracleAdapter.maxAge` from it.

# Part 3 — the per-position ladder (A8's owed pass over A4)

**Scope:** the A4 risk slider as built — `packages/shared/src/health.ts` `ladderFor` / `ladderBpsFor`,
`StrategyRouter.entryHfWad`, the withdraw gate, and the Solana twins. A8 was formally re-opened when A4
landed; this is that pass. Reviewed at `6cae5d9`.

**Method:** the ladder was evaluated against the positions the product can actually be driven into, rather
than the ones the worked examples assume — the two ends of the wizard's own slider, and the states an owner
can legally reach afterwards with the router's own functions. Two defects, both Medium, both fixed. Each
needed a product decision, which the founder took on 2026-09-13 (D9, D10).

| # | Severity | Where | One line |
|---|---|---|---|
| LADDER-1 | **Medium** | `contracts/src/router/StrategyRouter.sol`, `entryHfWad` | The entry record never moved after the open, so a legal withdrawal left a much higher entry's ladder in force and the keeper unwound a healthy position |
| LADDER-2 | **Medium** | `packages/shared/src/health.ts`, `ladderFor` | The rungs scaled with the entry without limit, so a high-HF opener had the keeper repaying and de-risking a position nowhere near liquidation |

### LADDER-1 · Medium · A legal withdrawal left the wrong ladder in force

**Where:** `contracts/src/router/StrategyRouter.sol` — `entryHfWad[account]`, written only by
`_recordEntryHf` on the open paths; `_withdrawGated`, which gates a withdrawal on
`REGISTRY.entryHfFloorWad()` alone.

**What it was.** The ladder derives from the health factor the position was opened at. Withdrawing
collateral is gated by the registry floor — 1.25 — and by nothing else, and it does not touch the record.
The two rules disagree about what a position is.

**The failing scenario.** An owner opens at HF 2.60 (30 % loan-to-value on cbBTC). Their ladder is warn 2.46,
repay 2.02, derisk 1.58, emergency 1.14. Later they withdraw 51 % of the collateral — allowed, because the
result is HF 1.27, above the 1.25 floor; the router returns success. The position is now **below its own
derisk rung**, so on the keeper's next tick it repays the debt and **closes the owner's liquidity position**,
seconds after the owner deliberately put it there. Meanwhile an owner who simply opened at 1.25 and sits at
exactly the same HF 1.27 is left alone. **The more conservative opener is the one punished**, and nothing on
the withdraw screen says so.

**Fix (D9).** An OWNER action that moves debt or collateral re-records the entry, in both directions:
`unwind` calls `_rerecordEntryHf` when `repaid != 0 || withdrawn != 0`. Three exclusions, each a defect of
its own if got wrong:

- **The keeper must not re-record.** Its protective rungs move debt too; a keeper that could rewrite the
  ladder it is judged against would loosen its own bounds on every rung it fired. The router tells them
  apart by asking the account: `OilskinAccount.keeperActor()` (new, `IOilskinAccount`) exposes the transient
  actor slot the account already keeps — the keeper's address during `execAsKeeper`, zero on every owner
  path and zero outside any call.
- **An unwind that moves nothing must not re-record.** Closing liquidity positions alone changes neither
  debt nor collateral, so the health factor is simply wherever the market has taken it; writing that in as
  the new "entry" would quietly relax the owner's chosen protection at the worst possible moment.
- **A position with no debt records nothing.** The record is cleared to 0, which the keeper already reads as
  "no record — run the registry floor's ladder", and the next open writes a fresh one.

**Known limit, deliberately not closed:** the owner's raw `account.exec` to the protocol bypasses the router
entirely and therefore bypasses the re-record. That is the documented exit hatch, it cannot be intercepted
without blocking it, and it is the same hole every other router-maintained invariant has. A stale record left
that way is *higher* than reality only if the owner withdrew through the raw path — worth a line in the
dashboard copy, not a contract change.

**Regression test:** `contracts/test/audit-regressions/EntryHfRerecord.t.sol` (7) — the 2.60-open withdrawn
to 1.27 re-records and emits; a repay raises it; the keeper's own granted `unwind` repays and leaves the
record untouched; an unwind that moves nothing leaves it untouched; repaying everything clears it to 0; and
`keeperActor()` is zero outside a call. The Solana twins (`withdraw`, `repay`, `close_position`) carry the
same rule, with the owner/keeper distinction free because `keeper_protect` is a separate instruction.
`deposit` is deliberately not re-recorded: it only RAISES the health factor, which leaves the ladder tighter
than it needs to be — the safe direction, and the one that costs an owner nothing.

### LADDER-2 · Medium · The rungs scaled without limit

**Where:** `packages/shared/src/health.ts` `ladderFor` / `ladderBpsFor`; `solana/…/health.rs` `ladder_for`.

**What it was.** `rung = 1 + (e − 1) × k` with k = 0.91 / 0.64 / 0.36 / 0.09, and no upper bound on `e`.
`StrategyRouter` bounds the recorded entry below (`EntryHfTooLow`) and not above; the keeper's
`resolveLadder` does the same.

**The failing scenario.** The wizard's slider is linear in the borrow and its left-hand end is "borrow
nothing", and the typed health-factor field has a minimum and no maximum — so a large deposit against a small
borrow opens at a very high HF by an ordinary route, not an exotic one. At an entry of 78 the ladder was warn
71.07, **repay 50.28, derisk 28.72**, emergency 7.93. A 36 % fall — which leaves the position at HF 50, fifty
times clear of liquidation — had the keeper spending the owner's USDC to repay; a 63 % fall had it closing
their liquidity position. Neither action protects anything; both cost the owner real money and their yield.

**Fix (D10).** `MAX_LADDER_ENTRY_HF = 2.00`. Above it the acting rungs — repay, derisk, emergency — take the
2.00 ladder's 1.64 / 1.36 / 1.09 (disarms 1.73 / 1.45 / 1.18) and stop deriving. `warn` is **not** capped: it
only notifies, and telling a conservative owner their position has moved a long way from where they opened it
is exactly what they asked for. Nothing at or below 2.00 moved — 1.25, 1.30, 1.55 and Kamino's 1.625 are
identical rung for rung and disarm for disarm, which the test asserts rather than implies.

**Regression test:** `packages/shared/test/health.test.ts` "D10: the acting rungs stop deriving above
MAX_LADDER_ENTRY_HF…" — the cap's own ladder, equality below the cap at six entries, the acting rungs pinned
to the cap's at 2.05 / 2.6 / 4 / 10 / 78 / 500, `rungFor(50.28, ladderFor(78))` proved to be `notify` rather
than `repay`, the acting rungs still biting at 1.63 / 1.35 / 1.08, and the integer twin agreeing at every one.
`solana/…/health.rs` carries the same assertions in Rust, with the shape sweep widened from 5.00 to 25.00 so
it crosses the cap. A consequence worth naming: `reserveFractionFor` reads the repay rung, so the
**cross-chain reserve also stops growing with the entry** — 5.2 % of the debt above the cap, where a 2.60
entry used to require 6.91 %.

**Observation O-2 is now closed** by LADDER-2; the §2b question the build plan left open for the founder is
closed by LADDER-1.

# Part 4 — the forecast service (A8's last owed pass, over A3)

**Scope:** `services/yield/src/forecast.ts` (422 lines), its inputs in `services/yield/src/sources/aave.ts`
and the cross-chain venue assembled in `services/yield/src/server.ts` `venueBorrowForServe`, plus the
`/v1/forecast` route. This is the step A8 named as the only one still owed a pass. Reviewed at `9a97ed2`.

**Method:** the forecast's defining property since D4/D5 is that it **prices everything and refuses almost
nothing** — so the pass asked the one question that matters for a surface like that: *can a number reach the
screen that the chain does not currently support?* The refusals were then read as a set, looking for a path
that is refused on one branch and priced on the other. One defect, Low; three things checked and found sound.

| # | Severity | Where | One line |
|---|---|---|---|
| FORECAST-1 | **Low** | `services/yield/src/forecast.ts` | A stale Kamino sample was refused *and* priced, so a cross-chain cell showed a borrow rate, a liquidation threshold and a pool depth derived from a read the service had already declared too old |

### FORECAST-1 · Low · A stale venue sample was refused and priced at the same time

**Where:** `services/yield/src/forecast.ts`, the `venueBorrow` branch of §1 and the guard on §2; the sample is
built by `services/yield/src/server.ts` `venueBorrowForServe`, which returns a fully populated venue object
carrying `stale: sample.stale`.

**What it was.** The two branches of the same function disagreed. On Base, `rates.stale` pushes `rates_stale`
and the whole `else` block is skipped, so **no cell field is set** and §2's guard
(`rates !== null && !rates.stale`) keeps the position unpriced. On the cross-chain path the branch pushed the
same refusal and then carried straight on: `borrowAprNowPct`, `collateralSupplyAprPct`,
`liquidationThresholdBps`, `venueMaxLtvBps` and `poolAvailableUsd` were all assigned from the stale sample,
and §2's guard admitted it on `venue !== null` alone, so `ltvAtEntryBps`, `borrowUsd` and
`borrowAprAfterPct` were computed too.

This is the codebase contradicting its own written rule. `services/yield/src/solanaBorrow.ts:212` refuses a
stale sample and returns immediately, with the comment *"a read past staleAfterMs is refused and shows NO
number derived from it (Base's rates_stale rule)"* — the sibling view enforces on the same sample what the
forecast did not.

**The failing scenario.** The Solana RPC goes quiet, or a refresh fails, and the Kamino sample ages past
`staleAfterMs`. A user opens the strategy step with cross-chain cells showing. Each card renders its numbers
**beside** the refusal, not instead of it (`web/components/wizard/StrategyStep.tsx` — the refusal is an extra
line under the forecast, and the net percentage keeps its place on the right). So the user reads "borrow
−2.78 %", a liquidation price and a year's net, all computed from a sample the service has already decided is
too old to act on, with a red line beneath saying the rates are stale. Nothing can be *done* — `allowed` is
false and the deposit button is disabled — so this cannot move funds; it can only mislead a reader, which is
why it is Low and not Medium.

**Fix.** One symmetric rule for both branches. A `venueUsable` binding is `null` when the sample is stale;
the refusal and `cell.borrowVenue` (*which* venue refused is not itself a stale number) are still set, and
every derived field — including §2's `ltBps`, `maxLtvBps` and the rate-after-borrow — reads `venueUsable`, so
a stale sample now yields exactly what a stale Base sample yields: the refusal and nothing else.

**Regression test:** `services/yield/test/forecast.test.ts` "FIX FORECAST-LOW-1: a STALE Kamino sample is
refused and no number is derived from it" — the stale cell refuses `rates_stale`, is not allowed, still names
Kamino, and has all eight derived fields null; the identical sample when fresh serves every one of them, so
the test is about staleness and not about a field that is never set on this path. **Verified to fail without
the fix**: with `forecast.ts` reverted, that subtest reads `not ok`; with it restored, `@zyo/yield` is
179 / 179.

### Checked and found sound

- **The borrow curve cannot divide by zero.** `aaveVariableBorrowAprPct` divides by `uOpt` below the optimal
  point and by `10_000 − uOpt` above it. `sources/aave.ts:180` throws `AaveDecodeError` if `optimalUsageRatio`
  reads 0, so the first divisor is non-zero by construction; and if `uOpt` were 10,000 the second branch is
  unreachable, because utilisation is range-checked to ≤ 10,000 and the branch needs strictly greater. Neither
  divisor can be zero on any input the reader admits.
- **A pool that cannot fund a borrow yields `null`, never an optimistic rate.** Both
  `venueBorrowAprAfterPct` and `aaveBorrowAprAfterPct` return `null` on "more than available", "past the
  borrow limit" and "supplied ≤ 0", and the caller turns that into `pool_cannot_fund` with
  `bindingCap = "pool_liquidity"`. The existing tests pin it on both venues.

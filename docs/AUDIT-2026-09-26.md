# Internal audit — the perps module's money-moving code, read adversarially the day the keeper path and the rail landed (2026-09-26)

**Scope.** BUILD-PLAN Stream D step D7's first wave, over what D2, D4 and D5 built (`docs/PERPS-DESIGN-2026-09-25.md`
§3–§6): `contracts/src/venues/HyperliquidPerpVenue.sol` and its libraries, `agent/src/perps/` (the keeper's perps
path), `StrategyRouter`'s perps pair (`setPerpRecipient`, `burnToPerp`), and `scripts/perps-d0b-testnet.mjs`, the
founder's first testnet run. Method: the venue's own documentation read beside the code, the question being *what
does HyperCore do with what the venue sends*, since a CoreWriter action that HyperCore refuses is silent (design §5);
then each seam the keeper hands the venue. Not covered: D6 (not built), the web, the yield service. The pass that
covers D6 is still owed before the freeze.

Fix commit for every finding: **this one**. Regression tests: `contracts/test/audit-regressions/PerpOrderPrice.t.sol`
(5), `packages/shared/test/perps.test.ts` (the order-rule test), `agent/test/perps-policy.test.ts` (the minimum-order
block). Severities follow the 2026-09-13 ledger: High = protection that could not fire at all; Medium = protection
that could not fire in a reachable state or an action the design did not intend, within the grant; Low = a consent
boundary or a silent no-op the owner would not be told about.

Abbreviations: CCTP = Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol; HF = health factor; IOC = immediate-or-cancel.

## Findings

### P-1 · High · every order the venue sent carried a price HyperCore rejects (`HyperliquidPerpVenue._toE8Px`; the D0b script's `sellPx` / `buyPx`)

**Where.** `_toE8Px` scaled the precompile's mark (10^(6 − szDecimals): 15,389,417 = 1538.9417) to 10^8 and shaded
it by the band — `1538.9417 × 0.995 = 1531.24699150` — and that was the limit price of the IOC in `open`, `reduce`
and `protect`. The D2 suite pinned exactly those bytes (`153_124_699_150`). `scripts/perps-d0b-testnet.mjs` built its
`sellPx` and `buyPx` the same way.

**The rule, from the venue** (hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/tick-and-lot-size, read
2026-09-26 16:33 UTC; `VERIFIED-PERPS-FACTS-2026-09-14.md` §7.7): *"Prices can have up to 5 significant figures, but
no more than MAX_DECIMALS − szDecimals decimal places where MAX_DECIMALS is 6 for perps … Integer prices are always
allowed … 1234.5 is valid but 1234.56 is not."* 1531.24699150 has ten significant figures. The mark itself, as the
precompile gives it, is not a valid order price either.

**Failing scenario.** Any of them. The owner's `open` lands on the EVM, records the entry, emits `ShortOpened` — and
HyperCore refuses the order it sent; no position exists; the keeper's next read says NO_POSITION and the entry record
is a fiction. The keeper's `protect(2, 0, reduceSz)` at the de-risk rung lands, charges the grant's reduce budget,
emits `Protected` — and nothing is bought back; the keeper's `confirm` says "did not fill" and re-arms three times,
then holds; at the close rung it re-arms for ever, each time charging budget for an order that can never fill.
**No rung that closes size could ever have acted on the real venue.** D3's testnet run would have found it as
"the order did not fill", without the reason.

**Why the tests did not catch it.** The Foundry suite's `MockCoreWriter` records bytes; the precompile doubles answer
scripted reads. Nothing in the tree models what HyperCore accepts, and the rule was never read: the facts file records
the byte layouts and the scalings, not the order's validity. Rule 3's "probe every external contract" was applied to
the precompiles and CoreWriter's selector, not to what the order engine behind CoreWriter takes.

**Fix.** `HyperCoreLib.roundOrderPxE8(pxE8, szDecimals, roundUp)`: at most five significant figures AND at most
(6 − szDecimals) decimals, integers always allowed — a quantum of `10^max(2 + szDecimals, digits − 5)` in 10^8 units,
capped at 10^8. The venue rounds INSIDE the band: a sell's floor up, a buy's ceiling down, so no fill can lie beyond
the band the owner or the grant allowed. `packages/shared` carries the twin (`roundOrderPxE8`, `isValidOrderPxE8`,
`orderPxE8ForMark`, `orderSzE8`), the D0b script uses it, and the four pinned prices moved: 1531.3 / 1546.6 / 1855.9 /
2165.2. A band narrower than one quantum (about a basis point at ZEC's price) leaves no room to round inside it — a
zero band rounds a sell's floor above the mark, a price no bid meets; the wizard's default band (50) and the grant's
(50) are fifty quanta wide. **Regression.** `PerpOrderPrice.t.sol`: the page's own examples, the four D2 prices
rejected and their roundings, a fuzz over (price, szDecimals) that every rounding is valid, within one quantum and in
the asked direction, the venue's open and reduce end to end carrying valid prices inside the band, and the zero-band
case; the shared test pins the same numbers and sweeps 10^17 prices at four size precisions.

### P-2 · Low · an order under the venue's $10 minimum was sent into silence (`HyperliquidPerpVenue.open` / `reduce` / `protect`; `agent/src/perps/policy.ts`)

**The rule, from the venue** (the exchange-endpoint page's own error example, read 2026-09-26 16:33 UTC): *"Order must
have minimum value of $10."* Neither the venue nor the keeper's plan knew it. A de-risk reduce sized to one raw unit
(0.01 ZEC) is $15 at today's mark and $8 under $800; a reduce clamped by the fraction or the budget could land under
it; a whole short under $10 cannot be closed by an order at all.

**Fix.** The venue refuses by name — `OrderBelowMinimum(valueE6, minimumE6)` — in `open`, `reduce` and `protect`
(`_requireOrderValue`), instead of sending an order HyperCore drops; the keeper's plan lifts a de-risk reduce to the
minimum size when the fraction and the budget allow, drops it and says why when they do not, and at the close rung
says when the whole short is under the minimum and only the reserve acts. The dispatcher classifies the new error as
transient. **Regression.** `PerpOrderPrice.t.sol` (0.01 ZEC at $800 refused on `open` and `reduce`, 0.02 ZEC
accepted); `perps-policy.test.ts` "the venue's $10 minimum order" (a two-raw reduce lifted to three; a budget of two
refused; a two-raw short at the close rung tops up only, and says so).

## Findings recorded on 2026-09-26 by the D4 build, re-read here (no change)

- **The grant's budget is charged on the EVM receipt, before HyperCore acts** (`protect`, `RISKS.md` §23). An order
  HyperCore refuses or does not fill has spent reduce budget. With P-1 fixed the refusal case is the one this pass
  can name (a price rule, a minimum, a lot size — all now enforced before sending); the unfilled case remains and is
  the keeper's re-arm. Whether the venue should charge on a later read instead is a design change with its own cost
  (a second transaction per action) and is left for the founder with D6's grant copy.
- **The de-risk fraction is a keeper knob** (`PERPS_DERISK_FRACTION_BPS`). Not answered here; the question is design
  §9 item 6 and needs the wizard's numbers (D6) to answer honestly.

## Observations (no change; recorded so the next pass does not re-find them)

- **O-1 `withdrawToEvm` sends two CoreWriter actions that depend on each other.** The perp → spot transfer (action 7)
  and the spot → EVM `sendAsset` (action 13) go out in one transaction; the venue gates the second on the spot balance
  it READ plus the first's amount. If HyperCore processed the second before the first, the `sendAsset` would find no
  balance and drop — nothing lost, the USDC stays in spot, the owner retries. The order of actions within one
  transaction is the venue's to prove on testnet (D3).
- **O-2 `confirm` cannot tell a fill from a liquidation.** A reduce judged "filled" because the size fell may have
  been the venue's own engine closing the position between the send and the read; the next tick reads NO_POSITION and
  the episode ends either way. Cosmetic in the note, not in the action.
- **O-3 `open`'s entry record is written before the fill.** By design (D9: the record is intent); an IOC that fills
  in part leaves `entryOf.sz` above the live size, and the ladder derives from the entry DISTANCE, not the size, so
  the rungs are unaffected. The web's position page should show both numbers.
- **O-4 the router's `setPerpRecipient` check is only as good as the deploy.** Without `PERP_FACTORY` the record is
  the owner's word (RISKS §23). The check also assumes the HyperEVM factory's salt is `keccak256(abi.encode(owner))`
  — the same source file; a factory deployed from a changed source would derive elsewhere and refuse the true
  address. `Deploy.s.sol` cannot read HyperEVM; the founder's deploy runbook must pin the two addresses from the
  HyperEVM deploy's own output.
- **O-5 `fundCore` does not read the adapter's `paused()`.** The adapter reverts when paused and the account's
  transaction reverts with it; a named refusal would only add a read. Left.

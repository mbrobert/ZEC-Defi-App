## 3 · The trust model in one page

- **The user's account** (`OilskinAccount`, an EIP-1167 clone) holds every position. Its owner is the user's
  wallet, set once and immutable. It cannot be upgraded and has no admin.
- **Peripherals are opt-in per call.** A call grants the target no rights over the account unless the caller
  sets `Call.callback`; only then may that target instruct the account back. Rights are not transitive.
- **The keeper** may invoke only `(target, selector)` pairs the owner granted, bounded by per-period token
  budgets, an expiry, and a revocation the owner can fire at any time. It never receives user funds. Two
  grants exist in the product: `StrategyRouter.unwind` and, for a cross-chain account,
  `StrategyRouter.closeLpAndBurn`.
- **The registry owner** is a timelocked role that can register assets and point them at venues. It is an
  owner and our copy says so; it cannot move funds.
- **Third parties we depend on and do not control:** Aave v3, Morpho Blue, Aerodrome and the MaxFi/Snuggle
  engine, Chainlink, Circle (both as USDC issuer and as the CCTP attester set).

## 4 · Invariants we already assert, and the ones we want attacked

Asserted in the suite, with the test paths in `docs/AUDIT-SCOPE.md`:

- The router's balance of **every** token it touches is unchanged across every entry point (a delta, not a
  zero — donations are inert).
- No borrow can open below the registry's entry HF floor; the **venue** enforces it, so no entry point can
  skip it.
- A keeper plan uses exactly one root selector, and it is one the user granted.
- Every close carries a price band, every swap a quote-derived floor, and a quote must agree with the band.
- `closeLpAndBurn` burns only to the recipient the owner recorded, never a parameter of the caller's.

**Please attack these specifically:**

1. Any path where a keeper, inside a legitimately signed grant, moves more value than the grant's budgets
   imply — including through a venue the budget parser cannot see.
2. Any path where the router ends a transaction holding a token, or where a donation can brick an entry point.
3. The multi-venue exit: an account with books on two venues, one disabled, one paused, mid-migration.
4. The cross-chain burn: anything that lets USDC leave Base to a destination the owner did not record.
5. The entry-HF floor under rounding — the offered LTV is `floor(LT × 100 ÷ floor)` and lands within 10.6
   points of Aave's own cap on cbBTC.
6. Re-entrancy across the account → peripheral → venue → account path, which is the one place we re-enter.

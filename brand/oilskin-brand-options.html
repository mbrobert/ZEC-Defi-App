Five marks for Oilskin

Same brief for all five: the storm is real, the coat holds, the person underneath keeps working. No shields, no locks, no vault doors — protection by shedding. Each is drawn in the brick & linen system, judged at 72px, 32px and favicon size, on both grounds.

OPTION 1 — the incumbent

Shed Line

OILSKIN

The idea: a waxed shoulder in profile. One bead of rain sits proud on the line; run-off rolls away at the hem. It is the product in one picture — water lands, water sheds, the surface underneath never gets wet.

Why it works: ownable (nobody in DeFi has a "shed line"), calm rather than defensive, and the geometry survives every size — at favicon scale it still reads as a mark, not mud. The bead doubles quietly as "your ZEC": intact, sitting on top, never absorbed.

Watch out: abstract — it needs the brand voice around it to explain itself. First-glance read can be "brushstroke."

OPTION 2 — the character

Sou'wester

OILSKIN

The idea: the fisherman's storm hat — short brim forward, long brim swept down the back. The single most recognizable piece of oilskin gear ever made, reduced to two linen shapes.

Why it works: instantly literal — it says "oilskin" without a word of copy, and it puts a person in the brand (someone wears a hat), which matches the working-in-the-rain story better than any object. Warm, a little salty, impossible to mistake for a generic fintech glyph.

Watch out: the most figurative option — it can drift "seafood restaurant" if the drawing gets too cute, and the two-shape silhouette needs care to stay crisp at 16px. Keep it stern.

OPTION 3 — the monogram

Lapped Seam O

OILSKIN

The idea: an O built from two overlapping arcs — a lapped seam, the construction detail that actually makes an oilskin coat waterproof (each panel overlaps the one below so water can never run into a joint). Two tones of brick make the lap visible.

Why it works: it is the letterform of the name, so the mark and wordmark reinforce each other; the overlap quietly encodes "layers of protection, correctly ordered" — which is literally the protection ladder. Cleanest of the five in an app icon grid, no container needed.

Watch out: closest to generic — broken-circle monograms are a crowded field (it must keep the visible lap step and two-tone or it collapses into "another crypto ring").

OPTION 4 — the hardware

Grommet

OILSKIN

The idea: the brass eyelet on a tarp or coat hem, with the lash line running through it — the point where the cover is tied down and holds fast in weather. Drawn as a thick brick ring with an espresso line through the eye.

Why it works: hardware says durability without saying "security theater." The ring also reads as a coin at small sizes — a useful double meaning for a yield product — and the line-through- the-eye gives it a distinctive asymmetry most ring marks lack.

Watch out: the coin read cuts both ways (generic token vibes), and the metaphor — "tied down" — is about holding, which is one notch closer to the vault/lock language we banned than shedding is.

OPTION 5 — the story

Dry Wedge

OILSKIN

The idea: the whole scene in one square — rain falls hard across the upper left, stops dead at the coat's edge, and the lower right stays clean. The single dot in the dry zone is the position: your ZEC, untouched while weather happens above it.

Why it works: it is the most honest diagram of what Oilskin does — it doesn't hide the storm, it shows it, and shows exactly where the storm stops. Great as a motif: the diagonal + rain field extends beautifully into headers, empty states and the deck.

Watch out: six rain strokes at 16px turn to noise — the favicon needs a simplified cut (edge + dot only). Busiest of the five; weakest as a tiny standalone mark.

The squint test — all five at 22px

My call: keep Shed Line as the product mark — it's the only one that is simultaneously ownable, calm, scale-proof and already carrying the voice ("protection by shedding"). Take Dry Wedge as the brand's second image — hero art, deck covers, empty states — where its storytelling earns its complexity. If you want more personality than Shed Line, Sou'wester is the one worth a second round of drawing; it's the only mark here a person could feel affection for. Lapped Seam O is the safe monogram fallback; Grommet is the one I'd drop first — it pulls toward the vault language we deliberately banned.
